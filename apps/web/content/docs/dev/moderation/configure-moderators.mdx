You set up moderators on the same AdminCP screen as every other staff permission. This guide makes a user a moderator who handles blog reports everywhere and newsroom story reports only in some categories.

## Before you begin

You need the `staff_moderators` admin permissions **Create moderators** and **Edit moderator permissions**, or a root role. The content types you want moderated must have [reports enabled](/docs/dev/moderation/enable-reports).

<Steps>

<Step>

### Add the moderator

Open **AdminCP → Staff → Moderators** and choose **Add Moderator**. Pick a role or a single user and save. The new entry grants nothing yet, and the AdminCP opens its permissions.

</Step>

<Step>

### Grant report permissions

Keep **Restricted** selected. Pick a plugin on the left, open a module, and tick the permissions. For the blog, open **Articles** and tick **View article reports**, **Claim, assign and resolve article reports**, **Open hidden articles** and **Hide and unhide articles**.

A permission that depends on another one stays locked until its dependency is ticked. **Hide and unhide articles** needs **Open hidden articles**, so a moderator can always see what they hid.

</Step>

<Step>

### Choose where a scoped permission applies

A scoped permission shows its scope under the checkbox: **All resources**, or the number of selected resources. Here the newsroom plugin's **View reports** covers two categories and the management permission covers one:

<ImgDocs
  withoutBackground
  className="*:max-w-full"
  src={scopeEditor}
  alt="Edit permissions for Grace Hopper with the Newsroom plugin selected and its Stories module open. View reports shows 2 selected and Claim, assign, annotate and resolve reports shows 1 selected, each with a Change button"
/>

Choose **Change** to open the picker. **All resources** includes resources created later. **Selected resources** lists the family's resources with a search box, loading more on demand:

<ImgDocs
  src={scopeDialog}
  alt="Where can they View reports dialog with Selected resources chosen, Local news and Culture selected, a Search Categories box, and Cancel and Apply buttons"
/>

Tick **Apply to every scoped permission in this module** to copy the same scope to the module's other scoped permissions. Each permission still saves its own scope, so you can narrow one afterwards.

</Step>

<Step>

### Save

Choose **Save changes**. The moderator's permissions apply on their next request. If a resource no longer exists, the picker marks it **Removed**. Take it out before saving.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Check the result

Sign in as the moderator. The user menu now has a **Moderator CP** link to `/modcp/reports`, and the reports board shows blog reports plus newsroom story reports from the two selected categories only. See [Process reports](/docs/dev/moderation/process-reports).

## When saving is refused

The form checks scopes before it sends them, and the API checks them again. Each problem is shown under the permission it belongs to:

| Message                                                    | What to change                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Choose where *permission* applies                          | Open **Change** and pick **All resources** or some resources   |
| Pick at least one resource for *permission*                | A selection cannot be empty                                    |
| *Permission* reaches further than *dependency*             | Narrow the permission, or widen its dependency first           |
| *Permission* names resources that no longer exist          | Remove the resources marked **Removed**                        |

You can only grant what you hold yourself. An administrator whose own moderator permissions cover two categories cannot give a third one, or **All resources**, to somebody else. Only a root administrator can grant **Unrestricted**. Changing a role's permissions or a user's roles takes effect immediately. VitNode clears the cached permissions of every affected user.
