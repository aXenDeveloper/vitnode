Payment providers deliver webhooks at least once, sometimes twice, sometimes
late, sometimes out of order. A browser can close mid-checkout. A plugin's
handler can throw. Payments is built so none of that loses a payment or grants
something twice.

## Webhooks: verify, store, queue, then answer

`POST /api/@vitnode/core/payments/webhooks/stripe` needs no login; the Stripe
signature is its authentication.

1. **Bounded body.** Anything over 512 KB is refused before it is read.
2. **Raw bytes.** The signature is checked over the exact bytes received -
   never over re-serialised JSON, which would not match.
3. **Normalized.** The adapter reduces the event to _which object changed_
   (`checkout`, `invoice`, `subscription` or `payment`) and drops the rest. The
   raw payload is never stored or logged.
4. **One transaction.** The event row goes into `core_payments_webhook_events`
   and its job into the queue together.
5. **Then 200.** If storing fails, Stripe gets a 5xx and delivers again.

The event id is unique per provider and test/live scope, so a repeated delivery
finds its row and queues nothing.

## Processing re-reads the provider

The queue job does not trust the event's snapshot. It fetches the checkout,
invoice, subscription or payment from Stripe and applies _that_. So:

- Two different events about one payment (`checkout.session.completed` and
  `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`) produce one paid purchase.
- An older event processed last still sees the current state.
- Statuses only move forward: nothing moves a paid purchase back, and a
  renewal's paid period only ever grows.

## Four kinds of state

| State        | Values                                                                 |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Checkout     | creating, unknown, open, complete, expired, failed                     |
| Payment      | awaiting payment, processing, paid, failed, expired, canceled          |
| Fulfillment  | not started, pending, fulfilled, failed                                |
| Subscription | incomplete, active, past due, unpaid, paused, canceled, expired unpaid |

Refunds and disputes are tracked beside them. A failed fulfillment never makes
a paid payment look failed - the purchase is "Paid, delivery delayed".

## Idempotent checkout

- The browser sends an idempotency key; the server scopes it to the buyer and
  refuses it for a different request.
- A per-buyer, per-offer database lock plus a unique index allow one unfinished
  purchase per offer, so duplicate clicks get the same checkout.
- The purchase and its checkout attempt are stored **before** Stripe is called,
  and Stripe is called **outside** any transaction with a key stored on the
  attempt. A timeout leaves the attempt `unknown`; a retry replays the same key
  and Stripe returns the same checkout instead of a second one.
- Return URLs are built on the server from the offer's `returnPath` and the
  configured web origin. Nothing in the request can redirect elsewhere.

## Fulfillment runs once per effect

Each business effect - `grant`, `refund:<id>`, `subscription` - is one row in
`core_payments_fulfillments`, unique per purchase. The job locks the row, runs
your handler with a transaction, and marks it completed in the same
transaction.

- Handler throws: the transaction rolls back (your writes too), the row is
  marked failed, the payment stays paid, and the queue retries with backoff.
- A retry after success finds the row completed and does nothing.
- Offer missing (plugin disabled, offer removed): marked failed with a clear
  message. Nothing else is granted in its place.

Core guarantees your handler's database writes happen once. Side effects
outside the transaction (an email, an external API) can repeat on a retry -
make them idempotent or move them to a queue job of their own.

## Reconciliation

The `payments-reconcile` cron job runs every five minutes and looks at small,
indexed batches of work that may have missed a webhook:

- unfinished checkouts after their window closes,
- payments stuck in processing,
- subscriptions just after their period ends (renewal due) or while past due,
- fulfillment and webhook jobs that have not moved for 30 minutes.

Each row schedules its own next check, so the job never walks the whole
history. Processed webhook rows are deleted after `webhookRetentionDays`
(30 by default); failed ones stay until fixed.

## Retrying from the AdminCP

**AdminCP → Payments** shows totals per currency, work that needs attention and
whether the queue worker is running. Staff with `payments: can_retry` can queue
a failed webhook event or fulfillment again - through the normal queue, and
without any risk of granting twice.
